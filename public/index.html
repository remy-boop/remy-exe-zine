▸ WHY THIS EXISTS

When calculators arrived, people said they would make us dumb. They didn't. What they did was move the bar. The interesting question stopped being can you do arithmetic and became what are you using arithmetic to figure out.

AI is doing the same thing. The question is no longer whether you can make something. Anyone can ask a chatbot to write code, generate an image, draft a strategy doc. That's the floor. The bar that actually matters now is whether you used the capability to do something worth showing.

Most people aren't crossing it. Most people are uploading their face and asking for the Ghibli version. Fine for a Tuesday.

What AI actually does, and this is the part the doomers won't say, is expose who has taste. Who has judgment. Who can take a vague idea and finish it. Who has the patience to debug something boring at 11pm on a Tuesday because they care how it lands. That stuff used to be invisible because it was bottlenecked by execution. You needed engineers, designers, years of practice, a team. Now the bottleneck moved up the stack. The bottleneck is whether you have anything to say.

A few years ago there was a job title in advertising called creative technologist: someone who could think in concepts and ship the thing. It was rare because it required two skill sets that almost never live in the same person. AI didn't make that role obsolete. It made it widely accessible to anyone who already had the harder half. Everything else can be borrowed.

I am neither a programmer nor a designer by career, and I don't have the full expertise of either area. What I am is a Product Management leader with over ten years of being decisive about what's worth building and ruthless about what isn't. That used to be the part of the job that mattered. Now it's the part you can run on its own.

So this site is where I document the journey of building real things with AI as a collaborator. Each chapter is a real project, not a demo, not a prompt. They are the proof that the argument works. More will follow.

The bar moved. This is what crossing it looks like.
