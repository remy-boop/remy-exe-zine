<Panel id="p-origin" num="01" title="LEVEL 1 START" open>

Across the app industry, store reviews are one of the highest-leverage feedback channels a product has. They show up directly in the App Store and Google Play listings, they shape ranking signals, and they're often the first thing a future user reads before deciding to install. The catch is that responding to them at scale is genuinely hard.

The Customer Support team has been carrying all four of those weights at once. Multiple stores, multiple markets (Mexico and the US as the biggest, with reviews in both English and Spanish), and a steady weekly volume that grows with the user base. The team has been doing the thoughtful, hand-typed replies the brand deserves, but the math gets impossible past a certain queue size.

At the same time, the brand has spent years building exactly the kind of material that should make replies easier. Brand voice docs. Tone-by-market guidance. Escalation rules. Standard responses for the most common topics. All of it lives in shared docs that any human reading them could write a great reply.

<PullQuote>
So the real question wasn't *"how do we reply faster"*, it was *"why is a human still typing every reply from scratch when the playbook is already written?"*
</PullQuote>

The idea: feed that knowledge base into AI, let it draft a first response per review, and put a Slack approval flow on top so the Customer Support team can approve, edit, or skip with one click. Keep the judgment human. Hand the typing to the machine.

</Panel>

<Panel id="p-fix" num="02" title="BOSS FIGHT">

How do you give the Customer Support team autonomy and control instead of replacing them with a black box that posts replies for them? Replacement was off the table from day one. Auto-posting AI replies without a human review would have eroded the trust the bot was supposed to build.

The win condition was different: build a tool that does 80% of the work so the 20% that still needs human judgment (tone calls, escalations, edits) gets the attention it deserves.

<div class="fix-grid">
  <div class="fix-card before">
    <span class="fix-tag">✗ BEFORE</span>
    Open App Store Connect or Play Console. Re-read the review. Translate to the right language. Watch the character counter. Submit. Repeat 40 times.
    <br/><br/>
    <b>Per review:</b> 3 to 8 minutes of focused work. High cognitive load. Inconsistent voice across teammates. No batch handling.
  </div>
  <div class="fix-card after">
    <span class="fix-tag">✓ AFTER</span>
    A draft appears in Slack. On-brand. In the reviewer's language. Under the character limit. Agent reads, hits Approve or Edit. Card updates to show who sent what.
    <br/><br/>
    <b>Per review:</b> under 30 seconds. No context switch. Consistent tone across the team. Triage and ship in one place.
  </div>
</div>

The autonomy showed up in three product decisions:

<b>Fetch on demand, not always-on.</b> The Customer Support team types <code>/fetch-reviews</code> with the params they want (store, country, ratings, lookback). They decide when to triage, what to triage, how much to triage. The bot doesn't push, it pulls when asked.

<b>Edit always available.</b> Every card has Edit, not just Approve. If the draft is 90% right, the agent fixes the last 10% in a modal and submits. The bot is a starting point, not an answer.

<b>The voice rules are the team's, not the bot's.</b> Tone, escalation phrases, language, brand do's and don'ts, all live in a <code>knowledge-base.md</code> file Claude reads on every draft. Update the file, the voice updates everywhere. The team owns the output even when the machine writes it.

<Gotcha tag="⚠ GOTCHA · APP STORE TERRITORY FILTER">
The App Store Connect API accepts a <code>filter[territory]</code> query param, documents it, and returns 0 results when you use it. Fix: remove the param, paginate fully, filter client-side via <code>attributes.territory</code>. Google Play had no equivalent issue, which made the silence on Apple's side especially confusing for an afternoon.
</Gotcha>

<Gotcha tag="⚠ GOTCHA · MISSING_SCOPE WAS SLACK, NOT THE STORES">
Approve clicks were failing with a <code>missing_scope</code> error. First instinct: store-side API key roles, since that's where the reply gets posted. Wrong. The error was actually on Slack's side. Updating a posted card after a button click needs <code>channels:history</code>, <code>groups:history</code>, and <code>im:history</code>, scopes that weren't in the original OAuth set. I figured it out by adding a <code>console.log</code> right before the store API call. When the log showed clean data going out and the store call *succeeding*, the failure had to be the Slack-side update path. Lesson: log before you guess.
</Gotcha>

<Win tag="✓ WIN · API-LEVEL REPLY CHECK SHIPPED">
Early on, the bot only checked the local DB to decide if a review was already answered. That meant manual replies posted directly in Play Console or App Store Connect would still surface in Slack on the next fetch, doubling work and confusing the team. I closed that loop by checking <code>DeveloperComment</code> on Google Play and <code>customerReviewResponse</code> on Apple at fetch time. Reviews replied to anywhere, by anyone, now drop out of the queue before the bot ever drafts a response.
</Win>

<div class="stack-section">
  <div class="stack-label">▸ STEP-BY-STEP WALKTHROUGH</div>

  End-to-end flow from slash command to reply posted in the store.

  <div class="walkthrough">
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">1</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">CS AGENT RUNS /FETCH-REVIEWS IN SLACK</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Command typed in <code>#app-reviews-ai</code> with parameters controlling what to fetch.</div>
        <div class="walk-code">/fetch-reviews store:both country:mx ratings:1,2 days:7 max:2</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">ratings · star filter</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">days · lookback (≤30)</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">store · google_play / app_store / both</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">country · ISO code</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">max · 1-100, default 20</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">2</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">SLACK SENDS THE COMMAND TO RENDER</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Slack POSTs to the bot's <code>/slack/commands</code> endpoint. The server wakes up if cold (30 to 60s on free tier), then parses the params.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">Slack Bolt SDK</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">Node.js 18 + TS on Render</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">3</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">FETCH FROM GOOGLE PLAY</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Authenticated via Service Account JSON from Google Cloud. Reviews matching the ratings + days filters fetched directly from the API.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">googleapis npm</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">GET 200 req/hr</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">POST 2,000 req/day</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">reply: instant</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">4</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">FETCH FROM APPLE APP STORE</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Authenticated via short-lived JWT (RS256, 20-min expiry). Territory filter happens client-side after pagination, since the API returns 0 results when you pass <code>filter[territory]</code> directly.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">App Store Connect API</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">JWT RS256</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">GET ~3,600 req/hr</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">client-side territory filter</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">reply: 2-24 hrs</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">5</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">DB FILTERS OUT ALREADY-ANSWERED</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">The server checks <code>sent_replies</code> in Postgres. Any review with an existing reply is dropped. Only genuinely unanswered reviews move forward.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">Neon Postgres</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">sent_replies table</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">6</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">SAVE NEW REVIEWS TO DB</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Each new review is written to <code>reviews</code> with <code>replied=false</code>. A draft slot is created in <code>response_drafts</code>, ready for the AI response.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">reviews table</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">response_drafts table</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">7</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">CLAUDE GENERATES ONE DRAFT PER REVIEW</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">The server calls the Anthropic API with the review text plus <code>knowledge-base.md</code> as context. Claude writes a brand-appropriate response, matched to the reviewer's language. Reply capped at 350 characters (Google Play hard limit).</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">claude-sonnet-4-5</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">~$0.01/review</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">knowledge-base.md</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">350 char limit enforced</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">8</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">BOT POSTS EACH REVIEW CARD TO SLACK</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">One Slack message per review. Shows store, rating, review text, AI draft, and action buttons. The Slack message ID is stored in <code>slack_messages</code> so the server can route button clicks and update the card later.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">✅ Approve & Send</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">✏️ Edit</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">slack_messages table</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">9</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">CS AGENT READS THE DRAFT AND DECIDES</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Approve sends the AI draft as-is. Edit opens a Slack modal with the draft pre-filled. The agent edits and submits. The server enforces the 350-char limit on submission too, not just on generation.</div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="walk-step">
      <div class="walk-num">10</div>
      <div class="walk-body">
        <div class="walk-title">RENDER POSTS REPLY TO STORE</div>
        <div class="walk-desc">Slack sends the click or modal submission to <code>/slack/actions</code>. Render looks up the review, calls the store API to submit, writes to <code>sent_replies</code>, and updates the Slack card to *✅ Sent (by @user)*.</div>
        <div class="walk-tags">
          <span class="walk-tag">Google Play: instant</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">App Store: 2-24 hrs</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">sent_replies updated</span>
          <span class="walk-tag">Slack card updated via msg ID</span>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

        <div class="xp-learning-title">AUTONOMY, NOT REPLACEMENT</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">The product framing that stuck. Every decision flowed from it. Fetch on demand, not always-on. Edit always available, not just Approve. Voice rules owned by the team in a markdown file, not buried in code. Whenever I drifted toward "just let it post automatically," I caught myself.</p>

    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">THE BOT IS THE TYPIST, NOT THE EDITOR</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">The right division of labor. Machine does the boring 80% (drafting, language matching, character counting). Human does the 20% that needs judgment (tone calls, escalations, edits). Once that framing was clear, the UI almost designed itself.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
  </ul>
</section>

<section class="xp-section">
  <div class="xp-heading-row">
    <h3 class="xp-heading">▸ DEV SECRETS DISCOVERED</h3>
    <div class="xp-subhead">development learnings</div>
  </div>
  <ul class="xp-learnings">
    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">FULL-STACK FROM PM SEAT</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">First time I owned the full stack end-to-end as PM. Server setup on Render, Postgres schema and migrations on Neon, Slack app config and OAuth scopes, both store API integrations (Google Play + Apple), CI through GitHub pushes, environment variables, deploys. Things I would have handed off, I owned here. The shape of "what's actually hard about shipping" looks different from this seat.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">LOG BEFORE YOU GUESS</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">Most "is it our code or theirs?" debates can be settled by one <code>console.log</code> at the boundary, printing the exact payload going out before the third-party call. Stop guessing, see real data. This was the cheapest, highest-leverage debugging discipline I picked up on this build.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">USE CLAUDE TO DEBUG CLAUDE</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">Applied the log-first principle on the missing_scope bug. Asked Claude Code to add the boundary log before the store API call, redeployed, and saw clean data going out and <em>succeeding</em>, which narrowed the failure to the Slack-side update path. Five minutes from "we're stuck" to "it's the Slack OAuth scopes." The dev craft is knowing where the log goes. Claude just made it instant to put it there.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">TWO STORE APIS, TWO AUTH FLOWS</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">Google Play uses Service Account JWT, App Store uses RS256 JWT with 20-minute expiry. They look similar but are different enough that copy-pasting one to the other is a trap. Keep them in separate modules. Don't abstract prematurely.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
    <li class="xp-learning">
      <div class="xp-learning-body">
        <div class="xp-learning-title">ENFORCE CONSTRAINTS TWICE</div>
        <p class="xp-learning-desc">Belt and suspenders on every constraint that touches a third-party API. App Store caps replies at 350 chars. The first enforcement happens at draft generation, the second at modal submission. Without the second one, an edited reply could go over and the API would reject silently.</p>
      </div>
    </li>
  </ul>
</section>
